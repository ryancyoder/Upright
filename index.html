Proposal from this visit
Settings
Vertical exaggeration
Elevation views stretch height against distance so a shallow fall stays readable — a 6 ft drop over 200 ft is a flat line at true proportion. Turn this off to hold every section at ×1, where the drawing is to scale and an imported facade photo lines up. The V.E. slider is hidden while it is off; your per-view settings are kept and come back when you switch it on.
Preview column
The photo-and-details column down the left of the site map and every elevation view. It follows whichever pin you tap or drag. Turn it off to give the whole width back to the drawing.
Filmstrip
The row of photo pins and grade frames along the bottom, shown on the site map and in every elevation view. Off is more drawing, at the cost of the quickest way to find a particular shot.
Split screen in portrait
Stand the iPad on its end while the map is up and the screen splits: the section on top, the site plan underneath, turned to face that section. Slide a pin left or right down there and it moves the same way up here. Turn the iPad back and it closes. The Split button in the map toolbar does the same thing by hand.
Fold drawings between views
Show each drawing on the other view. A stroke drawn in a section casts its extent onto that section's cut line on the plan — exact, since the cut is a real line on the ground, though only the length survives. A stroke drawn on the plan appears in a section as the plan folded down, beaded just under the anchor line: it says where something sits along the section and nothing about how high it is. Nothing is stored either way, and neither can be edited from the view it is shown in.
Ground line in sections
Join the survey points along each cut, in order, so a section reads as ground rather than as scattered dots. The points are measurements; the line between them is an assumption — straight, so a dip between two shots does not show. It is never drawn past the outermost point. A run joining points well off to the side of that cut is dashed, since it describes ground neither of them stands on.
Eye height when shooting grade
How high off the ground you hold the iPad. A shot pointed at the ground gives its own distance from this — d = h ÷ tan angle — so a just-shot pin parks roughly where it belongs instead of on a fan. Chest height against eye height is a 30% difference in that distance. It is only ever a parking spot: nothing here changes an elevation, and once you have properly placed one pin the app measures your real height from it and uses that instead.
Object types
Every measured thing is an object with a type, and the type is a shoot order, not a label. Tell the app what you are looking at from the row of pills in the sighting overlay and it walks you through the shots: origin first, then the height, then whatever ground points the shape needs. The origin is always mandatory — everything starts on the ground, so every object gives the surface at least one vertex. There is exactly one height per object, plumb above the origin; it shares that pin, needs no pin of its own, and is never counted as ground. Its number is the difference between two sightings off the same pin, so the anchor cancels and the height does not depend on the datum — provided both shots come from the same standing position. A height that cannot be sighted is simply skipped.
